# Viator (Tripadvisor) teardown

Researched 2026-10-16. Basis labels: [documented] [reported] [inferred]. Fee, price-display and cancellation rows were fact-checked on 2026-10-16 (see Verification).

Access limits: viator.com returned HTTP 403 to page fetches and a DataDome bot check in the browser pane (not attempted). No live product page, checkout or traveler help page was read. Those points are marked "not verified (needs a live checkout)". Sources older than 12 months (before 2025-10-16) are tagged [>12 mo].

## 1. Snapshot

- Tours, activities and attractions OTA. 425,000+ bookable experiences from 70,000+ operators on a supply platform shared with Tripadvisor's own storefront. [documented, [Tripadvisor FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1526520/000119312526051281/trip-20251231.htm)]
- Audience is travelers with higher purchase intent, pre-trip or in-destination. Parent is pushing demand beyond the North American source market, mainly Europe. [documented, same 10-K] Locals are not a stated audience. [inferred]
- Syndicates to 4,000+ partner platforms such as Booking.com and Expedia. [documented, [operator guide](https://operatorresources.viator.com/about-us/)]

## 2. Fees and business model

| Item | Finding | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Merchant of record on Viator's own booking system; GAAP agent for "substantially all" of these transactions, so revenue is commission, recognized when the experience occurs. On partner-site sales (a much smaller share) Viator is not merchant of record, is still an agent, and recognizes the commission at booking, net of estimated cancellations. | [documented, [10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1526520/000119312526051281/trip-20251231.htm), revenue recognition note and Item 1] |
| Cash flow | Collects the full amount at booking (commission plus the operator's share), holds the operator's share as a merchant payable until the experience occurs, then remits it. Under the supplier agreement Viator collects as the operator's limited payment collection agent, and its duty to pay is conditional on receiving the traveler's funds and on no chargeback. | [documented, [10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1526520/000119312526051281/trip-20251231.htm); [Supplier Agreement](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/317-supplier-agreement) s.1.11 and s.8, effective 2026-08-01] |
| Operator commission | Pay-for-performance: operators pay on completed bookings. No rate in any Viator source found: the agreement treats commissions and margins as confidential, and the Management Center uses a "Commission Rate" (value not stated in the help article) to convert between retail and net. Third-party estimates are uncited: not verified. Submission fee is $29 per new product (varies by currency), non-refundable, charged again after a rejection, and includes Launch Assist. Optional programs (Accelerate, supplier-funded Special Offers) lower the net rate for visibility. | [documented, [Supplier Agreement](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/317-supplier-agreement) s.6.2, s.11.1; [How Prices Are Set](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/389-how-prices-are-set), 2026-03-19; [submission fee](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/344-understanding-our-submission-fee-and-viator-launch-assist), 2026-06-08; [operator guide](https://operatorresources.viator.com/about-us/), undated, calls it a "one-time listing fee"] |
| Retail price | Operator sets a net rate, or suggests a retail price from which Viator back-calculates the net rate. Viator and partners have sole discretion over the retail price; that discretion is also in the 2023 and May 2025 agreements, restated 2026-08-01. Payout stays at net rate; Viator absorbs currency risk and marketplace-funded promos. Supplier-funded programs temporarily lower the net rate, and the net rate can also be adjusted for promotional programs. The undated operator guide still says "You control your own retail pricing," which conflicts with the agreement. Skift reads this as room for partner discounting. | [documented, [supplier agreement update](https://operatorresources.viator.com/updated-supplier-agreement-august-2026/), effective 2026-08-01; [Supplier Agreement](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/317-supplier-agreement) s.1.6 and s.1.7; [May 2025 version](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/354-supplier-agreement-old-valid-until-1-august-2026); reported, [Skift](https://skift.com/2026/08/21/viators-updated-operator-agreement-formalizes-a-power-shift-in-experiences-pricing-scoop/)] |
| Guest fees | No traveler service or booking fee found in the sources read. The API `bookingFee` is a contractual percentage Viator invoices to markup-model API partners, who are told to include it in the end-customer price; it is not a Viator.com traveler fee. Optional non-refundable "Cancel for any reason" fee at checkout, rolling out from the week of 2026-09-21. No rate or formula is published; the help article's checkout mockups show +$24.00 on a $317.48 booking for both extension types (illustrative, about 7.6% [inferred]). Not verified against a live checkout. | [documented, [pricing guide](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/pricing/), published 2025-07-15 [>12 mo]; [CFAR guidance](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/407-cancel-for-any-reason-operator-guidance), updated 2026-09-21] |
| Price display | One Toronto ticket test: Viator showed "$33.84, taxes and fees included"; the venue site showed $29.95 before tax and charged $33.84 after 13% HST, so the totals matched. A 2024 review also says Viator prices include taxes, and that tax is added only at the last checkout step on direct venue sites in the USA, not on Viator. Viator's partner guide says OTAs bound by California SB-478 must show full product charges excluding taxes, so tax display may vary by jurisdiction [inferred]. Viator's own checkout: not verified (needs a live checkout). | [reported, [Milesopedia](https://milesopedia.com/en/reviews/products/viator-reservation-activities/), updated 2026-10-14, affiliate-linked; [Destinationless](https://destinationlesstravel.com/is-viator-legit-review/), updated 2024-09-12 [>12 mo]; documented, [pricing guide](https://partnerresources.viator.com/travel-commerce/pricing/) [>12 mo]] |
| Tax and on-site fees | Mandatory on-site fees (park entry, port tax, equipment rental) go in structured exclusion fields; checkout shows "paid in destination" as a separate itemized sum, and products without the data may not be shown in key markets. If a traveler is forced to pay an undisclosed mandatory fee, Viator may refund the traveler and charge it back to the operator. Viator collects, itemizes and remits sales tax only where it handles tax: today a listed set of US states (5 from 2026-10-01, 11 more from 2026-11-02), where payout becomes "Net Rate exclusive of tax." Elsewhere the operator stays responsible for tax. | [documented, [supplier agreement update](https://operatorresources.viator.com/updated-supplier-agreement-august-2026/); [exclusions help](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/359-how-to-update-exclusions-with-mandatory-costs), 2025-08-19 [>12 mo]; [tax handling](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/396-when-does-viator-handle-local-taxes), 2026-10-14; [US states](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/398-us-states-where-viator-handles-taxes), 2026-10-15] |
| Payout timing | Funds held until the experience runs. The agreement (2023, May 2025 and 2026-08-01 versions alike) says a monthly remittance by EFT within 21 business days after the settlement period ends, US$50 minimum. Help center: bank payments monthly on the 7th to 10th working day after the travel month, PayPal weekly; a new supplier's first payment may be delayed 30 days. The operator guide's "usually every two weeks" matches neither: not verified. | [documented, [Supplier Agreement](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/317-supplier-agreement), Attachment 1; [payout help](https://help.supplier.viator.com/en/articles/91-how-and-when-do-i-get-paid), 2024-04-22 [>12 mo]; [2023 agreement](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1933762/000182912623007166/amphitritedigital_ex10-26.htm) [>12 mo]; [account setup guide](https://operatorresources.viator.com/create-business-account-for-your-tour-company/), undated; [operator guide](https://operatorresources.viator.com/about-us/), undated] |

## 5. Takeaways

- Viator is merchant of record and agent: it holds traveler cash and pays operators after the experience. [documented] That puts refund handling with the platform until payout. [inferred]
- Operator payout is tied to a net rate while Viator and partners control the shelf price. Price shown can differ by channel. [documented]
- A 24-hour free-cancel default covers most listings; stricter tiers exist for select, mostly multi-day or 24-hour-plus tours. Pay-later and a paid "cancel for any reason" add-on build on that default. [documented]
- Disclosure of exceptions and on-site fees draws scrutiny: TINA on strict terms [reported], the 2026 agreement on mandatory local fees [documented].
- Viator's own operator pages conflict on payout frequency ("every two weeks" vs monthly in the contract) and on retail price control ("you control" vs Viator's sole discretion). The signed agreement is the firmer source. [documented, inferred]
- Traveler-facing rules for weather, minimum groups and operator cancellations are thin in the sources found; operator pages carry the detail. [inferred]
